MediaNamaEternal

Deepinder Goyal faces medical community backlash over Rs 85,000 wearable 'Temple'

Pre-orders for 'Temple', a head-worn fitness tracking wearable priced at Rs. 85,000, opened on November 1, spearheaded by founder Deepinder Goyal. The launch quickly drew public criticism from doctors, notably hepatologist Dr Cyriac Abby Philips (known as TheLiverDoc), who pointed out that the company has not published scientific evidence to support its health claims despite earlier promises to do so before sales began.

The adhesive sensor, worn on the side of the forehead, tracks metrics such as 'Entropy'—measuring how hard the body works to shed heat—and 'BrainFlow', tracking blood flow to the brain. On November 1, Goyal claimed Temple is the most accurate heart rate tracker compared to flagship wearables even in high exertion zones. Responding to critics on November 5, Goyal defended the device in a post titled 'Dear Concerned Indian Doctors', stating that Temple is strictly a fitness and AI device rather than a medical tool, and that it holds necessary regulatory clearances. He noted that he has invested $30 million into the product and defended the pre-orders by pointing out a 14-day return policy.

The controversy highlights the blurring lines between consumer fitness gadgets and clinical diagnostic equipment. As Temple prepares for a release by the end of the year, the debate over required scientific validation for high-end wellness tech continues to unfold.

Why it mattersMarketers launching high-ticket wellness tech must balance bold performance claims with transparent, peer-reviewed scientific backing to maintain consumer and institutional trust. Ignoring calls for clinical validation from industry experts can spark severe public backlashes that threaten brand credibility.

Inc42Kissht

Kissht Secures Board Approval to Raise ₹832 Crore via Preferential Issue

Listed non-banking financial company Kissht has secured board approval to raise up to ₹832.2 crore via a preferential issue of equity shares. According to an exchange filing, the lending tech platform will issue 2.6 crore fully paid-up equity shares at ₹314.11 per share to 34 identified non-promoter investors on a private placement basis. The allotment remains subject to regulatory and shareholder approval, for which an extraordinary general meeting has been convened. Kissht has not yet disclosed how it plans to deploy the new funds.

The preferential issue welcomes an array of institutional and individual investors to Kissht’s cap table. Axis Mutual Fund schemes—Flexi Cap, Multi Cap, and Small Cap—are together set to acquire shares worth nearly ₹200 crore, while the Massachusetts Institute of Technology will invest ₹85 crore across two entities. Other notable participants include Ashoka WhiteOak Capital entities (nearly ₹75 crore), Envision Capital (₹40 crore), First Bridge (₹20 crore), Groww, 360 One, Alchemy Capital, Bandhan Small Cap Fund, and Unity Small Finance Bank.

This fresh capital raise arrives just four months after Kissht's stock exchange debut, where it listed at an 11.7% premium on the BSE over an issue price of ₹171. Founded in 2015 by Ranvir Singh and Krishnan Vishwanathan, Kissht provides digital personal and business loans up to ₹5 lakh, loans against property, and health-related insurance. In July, the platform also received regulatory clearance to begin distributing mutual funds.

Why it mattersKissht's rapid follow-on funding underscores strong market appetite for digital NBFCs aggressively expanding beyond unsecured credit into broader wealth and insurance products. Performance and brand marketers in fintech must note how well-capitalised lenders are leveraging unified platforms to improve customer lifetime value through multi-product financial distribution.
