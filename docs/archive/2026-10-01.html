Measurement Gaps Keep ChatGPT Ad Budgets at Testing Levels

Seven months after OpenAI began selling ads in ChatGPT, persistent measurement gaps are keeping marketer budgets at a test level. Agencies report product-side gaps unusual for established platforms like Google or Meta. For instance, Accuracast ran a U.K. lead-generation campaign where form fills came in, but the platform showed zero conversions two weeks later. Other agencies noted severe click discrepancies and conversion data arriving 24 to 36 hours late, completely lacking demographic or prompt insights.

Compounding these performance tracking hurdles is advertiser hesitation around legal terms. Activating conversion tracking requires signing OpenAI's tracking terms, which many corporate legal teams reject over liability concerns and data unease. Unlike Google and Meta, where brands have spent years building trust, OpenAI has shown little room for negotiation, explicitly telling agencies it does not accept red lines from advertisers.

These combined trust and measurement hurdles are also dragging down the adoption of custom audiences. Until reporting improves and legal frameworks mature, brands are treating ChatGPT advertising as experimental rather than core media spend.

Why it mattersPerformance marketers must balance the temptation of emerging AI ad channels against severe attribution gaps and rigid platform terms. Brands should demand standardized measurement and clear data-sharing agreements before committing significant budgets to new publisher environments.

SBI Mutual Fund increases stake in Swiggy with ₹300 Cr open market purchase

SBI Mutual Fund has acquired an additional 1.18 Cr equity shares of Swiggy through open market transactions, spending over ₹300.6 Cr based on the closing price of ₹253.90. Following this acquisition, SBI Mutual Fund's total shareholding in the foodtech major reached 14.16 Cr shares, or approximately 5.1% of Swiggy's total equity, crossing the mandatory 5% disclosure threshold under SEBI rules. Domestic mutual funds collectively held a 22.8% stake in Swiggy as of June 30, 2026.

The investment comes shortly after Swiggy secured shareholder approval to cap its foreign ownership at 49.5%, earning the classification of an Indian-owned and controlled company (IOCC). This status enables its quick commerce arm, Instamart, to transition from a marketplace model to an inventory-led (1P) model, directly procuring goods from brands to improve revenues and margins. Brokerage Jefferies previously noted that this operational shift could drive an 80 bps margin upside.

Financially, Swiggy reported a narrowed net loss of ₹791 Cr in Q1 FY27, down nearly 34% year-on-year, while operating revenue rose 36.8% to ₹6,812 Cr. Instamart also achieved a key milestone by reaching contribution margin breakeven during the quarter. Swiggy shares closed 4.88% lower at ₹238 on the BSE following the updates.

Why it mattersSwiggy's shift toward an inventory-led quick commerce model changes how consumer brands negotiate distribution, margins, and direct-to-consumer visibility on Instamart. Marketers must adapt to tighter inventory management and leverage the platform's improving unit economics for high-velocity product launches.

Amazon Pushes for Long-Term Ad Measurement to Capture Value Beyond Standard Attribution Windows

Amazon Ads is pushing for a longer-term approach to campaign measurement, estimating that roughly a fifth of a brand's value on its platform materializes after traditional seven-to-30-day attribution windows close. Based on a study of over 2 million campaigns conducted in June, Amazon aims to make the case for counting a full year of consumer value. The shift comes as upper-funnel measurement, AI-driven shopping, and cross-platform competition from TikTok, Instagram, and YouTube draw ad dollars away from traditional search.

Since launching a long-term sales metric in closed beta in October 2024, Amazon has built out offerings like full-funnel campaigns and extended look-back windows in its clean rooms to track shoppers new to a brand over a 12-month horizon. Lily Tong, a director of measurement at Amazon Ads, noted that actions like searching for a brand or adding an item to a cart often translate into purchases over time, even if they fall outside immediate cutoff dates.

Marketers will soon be able to use clean rooms to check whether projected long-term sales materialized, providing much-needed accountability and valuation tools for brand advertisers.

Why it mattersThis development allows brand marketers to justify upper-funnel ad spend by capturing delayed consumer purchasing behavior on marketplace platforms. Brands can utilize these extended metrics to prove long-term ROI that standard attribution windows routinely miss.
