DigidayOpenAI

OpenAI measurement gaps and data privacy concerns keep ChatGPT ad budgets at test levels

Seven months after OpenAI began selling ads in ChatGPT, measurement gaps are keeping budgets at a test level. Agencies running campaigns report unusual discrepancies, such as platforms showing zero conversions two weeks later or reporting 100 clicks when internal tracking picked up only 20. Other issues include insights arriving 24 to 36 hours late without demographic or prompt data.

Beyond technical hurdles, legal teams are hesitant to sign OpenAI's tracking terms due to liability and data unease, particularly because OpenAI does not accept red lines in negotiations. This friction is also slowing the adoption of custom audiences.

Marketers continue to compare these early offerings unfavorably against established platforms like Google and Meta, which have years of trusted tracking infrastructure in place.

Why it mattersBrands should carefully evaluate tracking terms and measurement limitations before scaling budgets on emerging AI platforms. Establishing clear first-party attribution methods remains critical when platform-side reporting is unreliable.

DigidayAmazon

Amazon pushes for longer attribution windows to prove long-term ad value

Amazon Ads studies indicate that about a fifth of a brand's sales value on the platform arrives after the standard seven-to-30-day attribution window closes. Based on research across more than 2 million campaigns in June, Amazon is making the case for a full 12-month measurement window. The shift comes as upper-funnel measurement and AI shopping pull money out of search, while competing channels claim credit for sales finalized on Amazon.

Since October 2024, Amazon has been testing a long-term sales metric in closed beta that tracks new-to-brand shoppers and their initial actions, like brand searches or cart additions. It then estimates future 12-month value based on historical returns. Additionally, Amazon has introduced full-funnel campaigns and plans to extend look-back windows in its clean rooms so marketers can verify projected long-term sales against actual results.

These measurement updates aim to give brand advertisers a clearer dollar value for top-of-funnel ad spend that traditional attribution windows miss.

Why it mattersMarketers must look beyond short-term attribution windows to capture the true lifetime value driven by upper-funnel retail media spend. Validating long-term metrics will help justify budgets for brand-building campaigns on retail networks.

Inc42TVS Motor

Electric Two-Wheeler Registrations Rebound 12% MoM In September

India's electric two-wheeler (E2W) sector witnessed a strong recovery in September, with total registrations rising 12.2% month-on-month to 2.07 Lakh units, according to Vahan data. This growth reverses a downward trend observed over the previous two months and nearly doubles the 1.05 Lakh units registered in September 2025. Traditional legacy original equipment manufacturers, including TVS Motor, Bajaj, and Hero MotoCorp, dominated the market, collectively capturing around 61% of September registrations.

In contrast, new-age players experienced a mixed performance during the month. While Ather Energy recorded a 4.9% increase in registrations, Ola Electric saw a 3.5% decline. The broader electric mobility market also strengthened, with total EV registrations rising 64.6% year-on-year to 3.15 Lakh units, driven largely by E2Ws. This upswing coincides with the build-up to the festive season, during which automakers traditionally scale up inventory, distribution reach, and promotional activities.

Looking ahead, mainstream manufacturers are continuing to expand their EV portfolios and target buyers beyond early adopters with affordable mass-market models and wider retail networks. TVS Motor maintained its market leadership with a 26% share and is actively pursuing international expansion into markets such as Nepal, Kenya, Sri Lanka, and Indonesia. The segment's ongoing evolution points toward aggressive competition as legacy brands challenge digital-first startups.

Why it mattersThe strong September rebound driven by traditional OEMs indicates that mainstream consumers are increasingly comfortable adopting electric vehicles ahead of the festive season. Brands in the EV ecosystem must refine their festival marketing and distribution strategies to capture market share from both legacy and new-age competitors.

Inc42Fresh Bus

Fresh Bus FY26 Operating Revenue More Than Doubles To ₹63.5 Cr

Ixigo-backed e-intercity bus operator Fresh Bus reported a 2.4X jump in its operating revenue to ₹63.5 Cr for FY26, up from ₹26.3 Cr in the previous fiscal. The rapid top-line growth was propelled by an aggressive expansion of its electric fleet and route network. However, this scaling also resulted in the startup's net loss widening by 33% to ₹43.6 Cr, weighed down by an 81.4% surge in total expenses totaling ₹107.2 Cr due to higher lease costs, maintenance, and distribution overheads.

Despite the wider bottom-line loss, founder and CEO Sudhakar Reddy Chirra noted that the startup turned contribution-margin positive in March 2026, maintaining a current contribution margin of 15% to 17%. Fresh Bus currently operates 100 e-buses across 10 routes in South India with an occupancy rate of 85% to 90%. The company is targeting ₹180 Cr in revenue for FY27 while aiming to reduce its net loss to between ₹25 Cr and ₹30 Cr by focusing on larger fleets and improved customer stickiness.

Moving forward, Fresh Bus plans to double its fleet to 200 buses and expand its routes to 16 by the end of FY27. The startup has laid out long-term infrastructure goals to scale its electric fleet to 500 buses by March 2028 and reach 1,000 buses across southern, western, and northern India by March 2029. These expansion plans rely heavily on maintaining high repeat ratios on established routes and optimizing operational costs.

Why it mattersRapid revenue growth paired with widening losses highlights the heavy capital expenditure required to scale sustainable infrastructure startups in India. Marketers at mobility and transport brands must balance aggressive fleet expansion narratives with clear paths to operational efficiency and profitability.
