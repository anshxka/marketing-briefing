TechCrunch AIGoogle

Google Freezes Open Source Bug Bounty Program Over Swarm of Flawed AI Submissions

Google has paused its Open Source Software Vulnerability Rewards Program after being overwhelmed by automated, low-quality artificial intelligence submissions. The suspension took effect on October 1, with the tech giant announcing it plans to provide an update on the initiative in the first quarter of 2027.

The program, which pays rewards to external researchers for identifying security vulnerabilities in Google's open-source projects, was halted because the vast majority of incoming reports were invalid or riddled with AI hallucinations. Company engineers and open-source maintainers found themselves swamped by the influx of flawed data, fulfilling earlier industry warnings about AI-generated slop derailing bounty programs.

While this specific open-source initiative remains frozen, Google stated that researchers and participants are encouraged to direct their efforts toward the company's other ongoing bug bounty tracks.

Why it mattersGoogle's freeze illustrates the hidden cost of uncontrolled generative AI spam clogging operational pipelines. For performance and digital marketers, it serves as a cautionary tale on establishing strict verification filters before opening community, feedback, or user-generated submission channels to the public.

The selling shareholders saw vastly different outcomes through the OFS. Ahead of the public offering, AceVector raised ₹189 Cr from anchor investors, with Helios Mutual Fund and Taurus Ethical Fund being the only participating mutual funds. The firm has earmarked ₹132 Cr from fresh issue proceeds for Snapdeal's marketing and business promotion, alongside ₹50 Cr for technology infrastructure.

Founded in 2010 by Kunal Bahl and Rohit Bansal, the business adopted the AceVector identity in 2022 and encompasses the marketplace, ecommerce SaaS firm Unicommerce, and Stellaro Brands. Snapdeal currently focuses on value ecommerce, where fashion drives over 60% of business and 84% of customers originate from non-metro geographies. Financially, AceVector's restated net loss narrowed nearly 64% to ₹45.5 Cr in FY26, while operating revenue grew 29.2% to ₹510.3 Cr.
