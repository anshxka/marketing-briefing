ET BrandEquityAmazon

Amazon to Overhaul Ad Impression Metrics in 2027, Anticipating a 63% Drop in Reported Counts

Amazon Ads has announced a structural change to its impression-counting methodology across the Amazon Store, scheduled to take effect on January 1, 2027. Under the existing system, an ad impression has been recorded the moment an ad begins downloading, irrespective of whether the shopper scrolls down to view it. With the new approach, impressions will only be logged when an ad actually appears on a consumer's screen.

The revised standard applies to display, video, and audio (DVA) ad placements throughout Amazon's ecosystem, including homepages, search results, product detail pages, and order pages. Amazon projects that reported impression counts across these placements will decrease by an average of 63%, with specific variance depending on campaign placement mix. Media Rating Council (MRC)-accredited viewable impression metrics and attribution data will remain unchanged, and historical campaign data prior to 2027 will not be retroactively altered.

Industry experts observe that this measurement adjustment will significantly affect campaign reporting dashboards rather than real-world exposure. Because the same advertising spend will be divided across a smaller pool of verified on-screen impressions, reported click-through rates (CTRs) and cost per thousand impressions (CPMs) are expected to rise, prompting brands to reassess their historical efficiency benchmarks and placement-level investments without necessarily cutting overall media budgets.

Why it mattersBrands must prepare for a significant cosmetic recalibration of Amazon metrics as reported CPMs and CTRs increase when unviewed impressions are eliminated. Advertisers should audit their current placement performance to establish clean baseline benchmarks ahead of the measurement shift.

Meta Adjusts Recommendation Algorithms in India to Curb Violent and Sexually Suggestive Content

Meta has updated its recommendation algorithms specifically for the Indian market to curb the circulation of violent and sexually suggestive content. According to a company press release, Meta adjusted its systems based on direct user feedback in India to filter out material local users stated they did not want to see, successfully keeping more of such content out of automated recommendation feeds.

While Meta did not reference government directives in its announcement, the operational change follows sustained regulatory pressure from the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology (MeitY). Over recent months, MeitY issued an advisory requiring Meta's recommendation engines on Instagram and Facebook to diversify information rather than expose users to limited narratives amid political discussions around the Cockroach Janta Party (CJP). In July 2026, MeitY also summoned Meta’s president of global affairs, Joel Kaplan, after the platform briefly blocked a reel by Prime Minister Narendra Modi, rejecting Meta’s explanations as unreasonable.

The regulatory scrutiny has broader implications for Meta's operations in India. Government officials have argued that because algorithmic recommendation systems amplify content, Meta should no longer qualify as a simple intermediary, challenging its safe harbor protections under the Information Technology Act. Additionally, MeitY previously issued a notice to Meta demanding explanations over ads related to child sexual abuse material following a BBC investigation.

Why it mattersMarketers must prepare for shifts in organic reach and distribution patterns on Instagram and Facebook as Meta continues tuning its recommendation engines for Indian regulatory compliance. A tighter grip on algorithmic recommendations means brand safety environments may improve, but content discovery models could become more restricted and less predictable.
