TechCrunch AIAnthropic

Anthropic Proposes Federal AI Safety Bill to Block Models Capable of Causing Mass Casualties

AI research lab Anthropic is shopping a draft AI safety bill to U.S. lawmakers that outlines specific criteria under which government intervention could block the release of an advanced artificial intelligence model. The proposed legislation targets scenarios presenting a foreseeable, imminent risk of killing more than 50 people or causing over $1 billion in property damage resulting from biological weapons, cyberattacks, or a model evading developer controls. These catastrophic risk metrics mirror standards enacted in California's Senate Bill 53.

The draft bill outlines an oversight mechanism relying on independent evaluators registered with the U.S. Commerce Department to analyze safety risks. AI labs would be permitted to pay these evaluators, provided compensation is not contingent on the assessment results. This approach follows broader industry debates around governance structures, such as DeepMind chairman Demis Hassabis's earlier proposal for a self-governing body modeled after the financial sector's FINRA.

Should evaluators identify an imminent catastrophic risk, they would have 72 hours to notify the Commerce Department. The agency would then need to request an injunction from a Washington, D.C., judge to halt the model's release, expressly prohibiting any federal agency from blocking distribution unilaterally. An Anthropic spokesperson confirmed the draft reflects policies from the company's Advanced AI Framework.

Why it mattersAs foundation model developers actively shape compliance legislation, brand marketers will see clearer legal liability and safety benchmarks around high-risk AI deployments. Companies relying on advanced AI agents must prepare for independent evaluation protocols and stricter distribution safeguards.

Jio Platforms Launches India's Largest IPO as QIB Quota Books Out Within Minutes

Reliance Industries’ digital services unit, Jio Platforms, saw strong initial demand for its public offering today, with the qualified institutional buyers’ (QIB) portion fully subscribed within minutes of opening. Overall, the issue reached 58% subscription in early trading, logging bids for 11.48 crore shares against the 19.75 crore on offer. The institutional portion was subscribed 1.95 times, driven heavily by domestic financial institutions, banks, and insurers, while non-institutional and retail investor buckets stood booked at 12% and 10%, respectively.

The public issue follows an ₹8,109.8 crore anchor round that allotted shares at ₹1,119 each to global and domestic funds including BlackRock, the Government of Singapore, and the Abu Dhabi Investment Authority. Priced between ₹1,065 and ₹1,119 per share, the issue looks to raise up to ₹30,213 crore entirely via fresh shares, eclipsing Hyundai Motor India’s ₹27,870 crore issue to become India’s largest IPO. The issue values Jio Platforms at roughly ₹10.3 lakh crore (around $108 billion).

Jio Platforms intends to deploy ₹27,500 crore from the proceeds to prepay existing borrowings of Reliance Jio Infocomm, using the remainder for general corporate needs. As the issue heads toward its closing date on Friday, October 23, the company is pivoting toward expanding beyond basic telecom connectivity into fixed broadband, cloud services, enterprise products, and AI. With a base of 533.3 million telecom customers and 28.6 million broadband users as of June 30, 2026, Jio’s future growth depends heavily on monetizing this footprint.

Why it mattersJio's massive public market capitalization gives it unparalleled capital strength to aggressively build out cloud, broadband, and ad-supported digital ecosystems. For marketers, Jio’s expanding consumer base reinforces its role as one of India's dominant digital gateways for customer acquisition and data-driven targeting.
