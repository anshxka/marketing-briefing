Inc42Dark.ai

Privacy-Focused AI Assistants Gain Ground as Enterprise Data Concerns Grow

As individuals and enterprises increasingly depend on AI assistants to handle sensitive tasks, a growing trust deficit is creating momentum for privacy-first AI tools. While AI systems become more capable the more data they ingest, businesses are growing wary of exposing proprietary information, financial histories, and confidential files to external platforms.

In response to this tension, a new wave of privacy-oriented AI products is emerging. Startup Eternal recently launched Dark.ai, an assistant built around open-weight models and end-to-end encryption. Globally, tools such as Brave Leo, Duck.ai, and Proton Lumo are also tackling data handling concerns. According to Saakar Yadav, founder of legal research platform Lexlegis.ai, certain organizations require solutions that run models locally on on-premise hardware without an active internet connection to keep sensitive documents from leaving their systems.

Startups are currently testing several approaches to secure data, including running models on-device, masking identifying details, encrypting conversations, and restricting data access. The central challenge moving forward will be demonstrating that private, localized models can deliver the same context-rich utility as cloud-based giants while establishing a sustainable business model.

Why it mattersMarketers deploying customer-facing AI agents or internal analytics must prioritize data privacy to protect proprietary campaigns and maintain customer trust. As privacy becomes a competitive selling point, brands that adopt transparent, privacy-first architectures will stand out to security-conscious consumers.

Snapchat Reaches 250 Million Users in India, Making a Focused Push for Gen Z Ad Spends

Snapchat's user base in India has surged to 250 million people, up from 100 million in 2021, accounting for a sizable share of its global community of roughly 970 million. Despite this scale, Snap's ad business in the country remains in "phase one," according to Hussein Freijeh, the company's head for Asia-Pacific and the Middle East. While specific revenue figures were not disclosed, Snap reported that advertiser count has grown tenfold and spend per advertiser has increased threefold, identifying India as one of its top three growth opportunities through 2027.

Snap's pitch to Indian advertisers centers on capturing Gen Z attention through deliberate ad restraint rather than sheer volume. Freijeh points out that Gen Z consumers are generally 34% less attentive to ads, whereas Snap's platform design captures 22% more attention. The app allows brands to engage audiences through varied surfaces including Snap Map, its camera, content feeds, and sponsored chat placements.

To prove commercial viability, Snap highlighted campaigns such as Swiggy generating 60,000 transactions within two days and Domino's listing 1,600 stores on Snap Map. The company now faces the challenge of shifting core brand budgets onto its platform within a competitive and constrained Indian advertising market.

Why it mattersMarketers seeking high-intent Gen Z audiences can look to Snap's unique surface formats—like maps and direct chat placements—for measurable performance and commerce outcomes. However, media planners must weigh Snap's deliberate, lower-frequency ad model against the raw impression volumes offered by rival platforms.
