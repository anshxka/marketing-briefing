Why it mattersWithout a native ad revenue-sharing model on Instagram's long-form video, creators will remain almost entirely reliant on direct brand sponsorships for income. Inc42CoinDCX

ED Searches CoinDCX and Delta Exchange Over Alleged Forex Violations

The Enforcement Directorate (ED) carried out search operations across three premises linked to crypto platforms CoinDCX and Delta Exchange India in Bengaluru on November 4 and 5. The investigative action was initiated under the Foreign Exchange Management Act (FEMA) over alleged unauthorized cross-border transactions involving crypto token derivatives and real-world asset tokens running into thousands of crores of rupees.

According to the ED, Neblio Technologies (the parent entity of CoinDCX) and Excelium Technologies (operator of Delta Exchange India) facilitated spot trading, futures and options (F&O), and trading in tokenized foreign stocks via cryptocurrencies without requisite authorizations. The agency also stated that CoinDCX actively advertised these derivative and tokenized products despite lacking regulatory permission to offer them.

The agency's findings allege that user rupee deposits were converted into stablecoins, primarily USDT, and moved to offshore exchanges including Binance, KuCoin, Gate.io, and Bybit to bypass foreign exchange rules. For spot trades, CoinDCX allegedly routed USDT via an account tied to a Costa Rica-based paper entity. Delta Exchange India stated that it is cooperating fully and denied involvement in cross-border transfers of virtual digital assets, while CoinDCX had not released an official statement.

Why it mattersThe ED's action underscores the severe compliance and legal risks of promoting unapproved financial instruments and cross-border crypto derivatives. Fintech and Web3 marketers must ensure that all promotional claims and advertised products strictly align with domestic regulatory frameworks to avoid sudden operational disruption and brand fallout.
