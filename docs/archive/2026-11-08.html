ET BrandEquityPublicis Groupe

Publicis and WPP Clash Publicly Over Pitchless Agency Appointments and Media Reviews

A dispute between advertising holding companies WPP and Publicis Groupe has escalated into a public confrontation over pitch methodologies and how global advertisers select agency partners. The tension follows remarks made by WPP chief executive Cindy Rose during an October 29 third-quarter earnings call, in which she questioned the growing trend of brands awarding major accounts without traditional pitches, warning against "CEO handshake" deals and urging thorough due diligence.

Publicis hit back on November 6 via an open letter posted to Instagram, calling Rose's comments an insult to client intelligence. Publicis argued that opting out of pitches does not equate to bypassing rigorous evaluations. The agency network also turned the spotlight onto WPP's choice to sit out Coca-Cola's North American media review, openly questioning whether WPP's decision was driven by capacity or capability constraints.

The exchange reflects high-stakes account rivalry across the beverage sector. WPP has managed Coca-Cola work since 2021 via its dedicated Open X unit, but Publicis captured the beverage giant's North American media in 2025. With both networks set to vie for Coca-Cola's global media account in 2026, the contest was further complicated in September when rival PepsiCo appointed Publicis as its global media partner, ending a nearly 30-year relationship with Omnicom's OMD.

Why it mattersThe friction highlights how traditional, protracted agency pitch processes are being challenged by direct client appointments and custom network models. For enterprise marketers, agency selection is increasingly shifting toward proven capability and operational alignment over traditional pitch cycles.

Why it mattersThe reality of AI accelerating its own development means marketing tech capabilities will evolve faster than standard enterprise software cycles. Brand leaders must design adaptive, model-agnostic marketing stacks rather than locking into tools that could be obsolete within months.
