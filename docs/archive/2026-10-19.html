Durga Puja OOH Spends Surge Up to 30% Driven by Digital Formats and Pandal Expansions

Kolkata’s streets witnessed an aggressive advertising rush during Durga Puja, with festive out-of-home (OOH) advertising expenditure projected to rise by 25% to 30% in 2026 compared to last year. Brands capitalized on high festive footfalls, illuminated pandal facades, neighbourhood celebrations, and the city's pandal-hopping culture through conventional billboards, digital screens, and experiential installations.

Rajesh Radhakrishnan, co-founder and CMO of Vritti iMedia and Mindwave Media, estimated an even higher year-on-year growth rate of 30% to 40%. He attributed the surge to wider brand participation, expanding pandal sponsorships, increased adoption of digital OOH formats, and advertisers venturing beyond marquee city pandals into localized residential catchments.

While there is no formal audited figure capturing the total scale of Durga Puja outdoor advertising, trade estimates place Kolkata’s seasonal Puja hoarding trade at roughly Rs 150 crore. Across West Bengal, trade operators peg the broader festive outdoor advertising business at between Rs 800 crore and Rs 1,000 crore over the ten-day period.

Why it mattersHigh-impact regional festivals offer concentrated reach that brands can capture by pairing experiential pandal sponsorships with digitized outdoor formats. For marketers in India, expanding OOH campaigns into local catchment areas outside marquee hubs delivers deeper consumer engagement at scale.

Second-Tier Agency Holding Groups Face Pressure to Differentiate from the Big Three

New dividing lines are solidifying across the global advertising landscape as the 'Big Three' holding companies—Publicis, Omnicom, and WPP—consolidate their scale and market dominance. In response, a second tier of major agencies, including Dentsu, Havas, Horizon, and Stagwell, alongside independent networks like Brandtech Group, Dept, and Tinuiti, are exploring new alliances and brand revamps to remain competitive.

Industry consultants caution that these challenger networks are facing intense commercial pressure. Agency advisors note that not every large advertiser requires the sprawling complexity of the largest holding companies. Opportunities remain for mid-tier challengers that successfully blend holding-company sophistication and international reach with the agility and intimacy of independent shops, provided they carve out a distinct identity rather than chasing the same legacy accounts.

Why it mattersMarketers evaluating agency partners should consider whether the vast scale of top-tier holding companies outweighs the nimbleness and specialized attention of mid-sized challenger networks. As mid-tier agencies fight for survival, brands hold greater leverage to negotiate bespoke service models and senior-level access.
