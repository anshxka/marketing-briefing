Delhi court to examine Amazon's intermediary status in a copyright infringement Kindle case

A Delhi court is set to examine the intermediary status of Amazon following a copyright and publishing dispute involving author Vasundhara Prakash. The author has alleged that Amazon created and sold an unauthorized Kindle version of her book on its platform.

Furthermore, the petition claims that Amazon failed to take down the unauthorized listing within the mandated 72-hour window under the Information Technology Rules. This legal challenge directly tests the legal protections and obligations of major e-commerce and content platforms operating in India regarding copyright enforcement and takedown mandates.

The court's upcoming examination will determine whether Amazon's role as an intermediary shields it from liability in this instance. The outcome could set a significant precedent for how strictly e-commerce marketplaces must police digital content and adhere to statutory takedown timelines.

Why it mattersThis case serves as a crucial reminder for digital marketplaces and platforms regarding the strict enforcement of IT Rules and takedown timelines. Brand marketers and platform operators must ensure robust monitoring systems to mitigate liability risks.
