ET BrandEquityTAM Media Research

TV News Ad Volumes Drop 7% in Jan–Jul 2026, TAM AdEx Data Shows

Television advertising volumes in the news genre slipped 7 percent between January and July 2026 compared to the same period in 2025, according to the latest TAM AdEx report. While overall news volumes contracted, the drop represents a deceleration compared to the 15 percent decline recorded during the same period in 2025 versus 2024. Across the genre, more than 5,100 brands remained active on television screens during the seven-month span.

The food and beverages sector led news channel advertising with a 14 percent volume share, followed closely by services at 13 percent and education at 10 percent. Industrial and building materials accounted for 8 percent, while BFSI and automobiles each took 7 percent. Overall, the top 10 sectors captured 81 percent of total ad volumes, with categories such as spices and two-wheelers demonstrating noticeable upward momentum in category rankings.

At the advertiser level, Reckitt Benckiser (India) retained the top spot, followed by Patanjali Ayurved. GCMMF (Amul) climbed to third place from fifth, while SBS Biotech held fourth. LIC of India and Godrej Consumer Products also advanced up the rankings to claim fifth and sixth spots respectively, as the top ten advertisers collectively commanded one-fifth of all TV news ad inventory.

Why it mattersEven as TV news ad volumes face a steady correction, leading FMCG and retail brands continue to rely on the genre for sustained mass presence. Marketers evaluating linear TV should watch shifting category mixes, as regional and niche advertisers like spices and vocational institutes take up inventory vacated by larger players.

ET BrandEquityNasscom

Only 13% of Indian Enterprises Have Scaled Voice AI Deployments, Finds Nasscom

Voice AI adoption across Indian enterprises is transitioning from experimentation to production, but enterprise-wide deployment remains limited. According to the 'India Voice AI Readiness 2026' report by Nasscom and Krisp, only 13% of organisations have scaled Voice AI across multiple customer journeys. More than half remain in early phases, with 23% exploring the technology, 32% running proof-of-concept projects, and 31% operating in limited production. Furthermore, just 9% of organisations use Voice AI to handle more than half of their eligible customer interactions.

The study, which surveyed 140 organisations including BPM providers, GCCs, and enterprises, indicates that primary adoption drivers are cost reduction and operational efficiency. About 61% of respondents achieved a reduction of over 20% in cost per eligible interaction, while nearly two-thirds observed a 10% to 30% drop in average handling time (AHT). Financial returns are realised relatively quickly, with 68% of organisations reporting returns on Voice AI investments within six to 24 months.

However, scaling remains hindered by legacy systems, enterprise integration hurdles, and multilingual accuracy. The report highlights that organizations predominantly assess deployments through operational metrics: 45% measure impact only for selected rollouts, and just 14% track broader business outcomes comprehensively. Nasscom recommends evaluating deployments through completed tasks, customer satisfaction, human escalation, and end-to-end resolution rather than mere call volume.

Why it mattersWhile Voice AI delivers clear operational cost savings, marketers must evaluate its impact on customer satisfaction rather than relying solely on handle-time metrics. Addressing multilingual complexities and establishing smooth handoffs to human agents remain critical to safeguarding brand equity.
