ET BrandEquityGodrej Enterprises Group

Godrej Enterprises tests microdramas on Instagram to unify consumer product marketing

Godrej Enterprises Group has launched an eight-episode Instagram microdrama series titled '2nd Gen Kumars' to test episodic storytelling as a brand-building vehicle. The show weaves multiple consumer businesses—including digital locks, home lockers, furniture, and home appliances—into a single domestic storyline focused on modern Indian family life. The concept was developed in-house, with Bonfire Stories Collective handling creative development and production.

The experiment aims to discover whether serialized short-form fiction can drive measurable business outcomes rather than just passive views. Godrej plans to track brand consideration and search metrics to evaluate performance. Sumeet Bhojani, head of brand and strategic insights at Godrej Enterprises Group, stated that the group wanted to be early on the learning curve of what it views as a promising format.

The test comes amid significant growth in India's microdrama consumption. A report by Redseer Strategy Consultants found that nearly one in two surveyed consumers engage with microdrama apps daily, and 84 percent discover the format via social media, short-form video, or word of mouth. The survey of 600 consumers also noted that 49 percent watched microdramas daily and 29 percent watched four to six times weekly, pushing total domestic consumption to the equivalent of roughly 2,100 years of viewing.

Why it mattersMicrodramas offer multi-category brands a contextual way to showcase different product verticals inside one cohesive narrative without resorting to standard ad interruptions. If tracking search and consideration proves successful, episodic social content could shift from an experimental engagement tactic to a quantifiable mid-funnel format.

ET BrandEquityThe Coca-Cola Company

Coca-Cola Retains WPP for Global Media, Shifts North America to Omnicom

The Coca-Cola Company has completed a review of its global marketing operating model, appointing Omnicom to manage media duties in North America while retaining WPP Open X across most international markets. The phased transition begins on December 1. Under the new arrangement, WPP will manage media globally excluding North America, Japan, and South Korea, with Dentsu maintaining its existing media mandate in the latter two markets as they were not part of the review.

WPP Open X will also remain Coca-Cola's global creative partner, an assignment that was excluded from the review. Coca-Cola stated that the reorganization is designed to advance its marketing capabilities through deeper integration of data, technology, and artificial intelligence (AI), improving the company's speed in responding to changing consumer behaviour.

WPP has served as a cornerstone of Coca-Cola's marketing network since the creation of the dedicated Open X model in 2021, operating across more than 200 countries and territories. Moving forward, WPP will continue delivering creative, media, creator marketing, and commerce services powered by its Open Intelligence data platform, which Coca-Cola highlighted alongside preparations for the FIFA World Cup 2026.

Why it mattersThe decision shows how large advertisers are separating key media regions to tap specialized expertise while preserving centralized data and creative infrastructure. For marketers, balancing consolidated tech stacks with flexible, market-specific agency appointments remains a viable strategy for scale and agility.

ET BrandEquityAdvertising & media spend

Shah Rukh Khan Tops TV Endorsements as Celebrity Commercial Volumes Drop 17%

Shah Rukh Khan ranked as the most visible celebrity in television advertising between January and September 2026, securing a 10 percent share of celebrity-led ad volumes, according to TAM AdEx's Celebrity Endorsement Report. Former Indian cricket captain M S Dhoni placed second with an 8 percent share, followed by cricketer Jasprit Bumrah at 3 percent. Actors Ananya Panday and Ranbir Kapoor each secured around 2 percent of volumes.

In terms of daily presence, Khan registered 26 hours of advertising visibility per day across TV networks, while Dhoni averaged 22 hours and Bumrah logged seven hours. Film stars commanded the highest overall share of celebrity ad volumes at 65 percent, with male actors accounting for 38 percent and female actors contributing 27 percent. Sports personalities represented 28 percent of the celebrity endorsement mix, while television actors held 8 percent.

Despite high visibility for top stars, total television commercials featuring celebrities declined 17 percent compared to the same period in 2025, falling on the volume index from 100 to 83. Celebrity ads constituted 25 percent of all television advertising, led predominantly by the food and beverages sector at 25 percent of volumes, followed by personal care and hygiene at 18 percent.

Why it mattersEven as overall celebrity-led TV ad volumes shrink, FMCG categories continue to anchor their mass campaigns around a concentrated pool of top film and cricket icons. Marketers must evaluate whether the high premium commanded by marquee talent delivers sufficient differentiation amid contracting television ad presence.

Inc42Swiggy

Swiggy to Shift Instamart to Inventory-Led Model to Boost Margins and Operational Control

Swiggy has announced plans to transition its quick commerce arm, Instamart, to an inventory-led model, starting by mid-Q3 FY27. Following its recent attainment of Indian-Owned and Controlled Company (IOCC) status—with domestic ownership rising past 58%—the company expects the entire transition to take two to three quarters. Moving away from its marketplace setup hosting third-party seller entities will mirror the path taken by rival Blinkit, which previously shifted to an inventory model to bolster profitability.

According to Swiggy’s Q2 FY27 shareholders letter, the shift is projected to shorten Instamart's working capital cycle by 8 to 10 days and add 80 basis points to its contribution margin once fully completed. The model will grant Instamart direct authority over pricing, product assortment, availability, and day-to-day operations, streamlining logistics across its footprint.

The strategic shift comes as Instamart faces margin pressure amid aggressive competition. In Q2 FY27, contribution margin dropped to -1% of gross order value (GOV) compared to -0.2% in Q1, driven by heightened spends on pricing, customer retention, and network expansion. Adjusted EBITDA losses widened to ₹895 crore, even as net order value grew 34.8% year-on-year to ₹6,594 crore and the dark store network expanded by 69 stores to reach 1,240 locations across 133 cities.

Why it mattersFor brand marketers, Instamart’s shift to an inventory model means dealing directly with the platform rather than third-party sellers, enabling tighter joint business planning, predictable stock availability, and centralized promotions. However, with the platform tightening control over assortment and pricing to repair margins, brands will face tougher negotiations on trade margins and listing terms.

ET BrandEquityNicobar

Indian Consumers Pivot Toward 'Quiet Premium' as Quality Replaces Logo Prestige

Indian consumers are increasingly placing craftsmanship, superior materials, and retail experiences ahead of brand prestige when purchasing premium fashion. Rather than relying on prominent logos to conform to societal norms, shoppers are seeking unique self-expression through subtle quality. An example is premium retail brand Nicobar, which sells a kurta for around Rs 10,000 featuring only a discreet red stitch under the top button as its identifier, with buyers actively paying for the product's understated value.

This shift highlights an evolution from brand recognition to genuine product appreciation. Historically, brand logos signaled social standing and cost, often overshadowing the item itself. Maruti Suzuki encountered this dynamic when its premium sedan, the Kizashi, struggled because the badge was tied to affordability, eventually leading the company to establish Nexa as a separate retail identity. Today, while brand identity still carries weight, consumers are willing to assess brands on design and finish rather than relying purely on legacy names.

This domestic trend diverges from the Western concept of "quiet luxury." While global labels like The Row or Loro Piana cater to ultra-wealthy consumers avoiding displays of wealth, India's "quiet premium" represents a much broader consumer segment. Positioned between the mass market and true luxury, this expanding base requires brands to invest in elevated shopping experiences and product construction to earn customer loyalty.

Why it mattersMarketers must recognize that logo-driven status is losing ground to tangible product value, thoughtful design, and enriched retail environments. Brands operating above the mass market should focus their messaging on craftsmanship and materials rather than relying solely on brand heritage.
