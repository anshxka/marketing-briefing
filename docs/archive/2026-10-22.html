Anthropic Launches Claude Duet AI Agent for Workplace and Business Tasks

Anthropic has introduced Claude Duet, an autonomous AI agent aimed at professionals and small business owners to rival Meta’s Muse and OpenAI’s Dots. Powered by the Claude Opus 5.5 model, the tool is rolling out as an experimental research preview accessible via Claude on the web and through dedicated mobile applications on iOS and Android.

Claude Duet is designed to function as an active operational assistant across integrated software. It provides users with a morning briefing highlighting critical items across connected applications, tracks background activity throughout the workday, and only prompts users when an issue requires attention. Equipped with its own virtual browser environment, Duet can manage routine administrative work like scheduling meetings as well as complex workflows such as building cross-platform dashboards, drafting sales forecasts, prototyping, and writing web applications.

To safeguard critical business operations, the agent requires direct user approval before executing high-stakes actions like issuing payments or deleting files. Anthropic has also added a dedicated safety classifier to block the sharing of sensitive data such as credit card details. Anthropic notes that the agent is still an early version prone to misjudging urgency or misreading conversation threads, and access is currently limited to US-based Pro and Max subscribers via a waitlist.

Why it mattersAutonomous agents are shifting from passive chat interfaces toward proactive workflow managers capable of monitoring campaigns and reporting across business tools. Marketers should prepare for operational shifts where dashboard maintenance and routine administrative workflows are increasingly delegated to self-directed software agents.

TechCrunch AIRecursive

Recursive Releases Flint, a Lightweight Coding Model Largely Built by AI

AI startup Recursive has launched Flint, an open-source coding model designed to run directly on consumer laptops. Founded last year and led by former Salesforce chief scientist Richard Socher, the company developed the model in under four months using its automated research platform, the Eureka Machine. Recursive previously emerged from stealth with $650 million in funding at a $4.65 billion valuation from backers including Nvidia, GV, and AMD Ventures.

Flint uses a mixture-of-experts architecture that engages only 2.5 billion of its total 40 billion parameters at any given moment. This compact structure allows developers to execute code generation offline on standard machines, preserving proprietary data privacy and bypassing the token usage fees charged by cloud API providers. Released under an Apache 2.0 license on Hugging Face, Flint contrasts sharply with larger alternatives like Meta’s Muse Spark Open, which requires 816 billion parameters and at least a terabyte of memory.

Recursive reports that Flint generated approximately 131 tokens per second on a MacBook Pro powered by Apple’s M4 Pro chip, roughly double the output of comparable models like Google’s Gemma 4 and Nvidia’s Nemotron 3.5 Lightning. On Nvidia RTX Spark systems, speed exceeded 150 tokens per second. The company also reported that Flint surpassed competing models under 50 billion parameters on the SWE-Bench Verified software debugging benchmark.

Why it mattersThe rise of efficient, on-device models allows marketing tech teams to build and debug custom software locally without ongoing cloud computing costs or data leakage risks. This makes rapid prototyping and bespoke internal tool development significantly more accessible for performance teams working with tight budgets.

This reality is forcing gaming marketers to look past top-of-funnel metrics such as clicks, downloads, and cost per install. Instead, the focus is pivoting to player lifetime value, session frequency, community engagement, and monetization. Minu Lee, vice president and head of India publishing at Krafton India, pointed out that the maturing Indian market requires a transition toward sustained engagement and repeat play rather than pure player acquisition.

Why it mattersIn high-volume, low-CPI markets like India, chasing cheap downloads without a retention framework wastes marketing budgets. Marketers must tie user acquisition campaigns directly to post-install metrics like Day-30 retention and session frequency to build viable unit economics.
