India's Festive Ad Expenditure Expected to Rise by up to 15% Driven by Digital and Quick Commerce

India's festive advertising spending is projected to grow by up to 15 per cent this year, reaching an estimated Rs 60,000 to 62,000 crore compared to around Rs 53,000 crore during the 2025 festive period. According to Datum Intelligence, the festive window will account for roughly 34 to 35 per cent of India's total advertising market, up from 30.5 per cent in 2022. Sectors such as FMCG, e-commerce, and consumer durables are expected to lead the marketing push.

Digital channels are taking the lion's share of the festive wallet at 67.8 per cent, while television is projected to capture 16.1 per cent, print 10.7 per cent, and OOH, radio, and cinema combined will take 5.4 per cent. Industry leaders note that a fundamental shift is happening in budget composition, with commerce media, quick commerce, creator partnerships, and digital video gaining substantial ground as brands transition to a hybrid approach tied closely to consumer intent and transactions.

As healthy consumer demand supports buoyant festive advertising, brands are expected to continue refining their strategies to target immediate transactions. The ongoing evolution emphasizes aligning media spend directly with consumer intent through quick commerce and digital touchpoints rather than relying solely on traditional awareness channels.

Why it mattersMarketers must reallocate budgets toward high-intent channels like quick commerce, commerce media, and creator partnerships to capture shifting consumer demand. Transitioning to a hybrid approach ensures ad spend is directly tied to active transactions during the crucial festive window.

Omnicom Media Study Reveals 65% of U.S. Consumers Engage in Ad Avoidance Behaviors

Omnicom Media Intelligence has released a study titled 'From Tuned Out to Leaned In: How to Combat Ad Avoidance,' highlighting that 65% of U.S. consumers avoid advertising to some degree. The research, which surveyed 1,136 adults in August and tested various video-based creative executions with firm eye square, found that one in four consumers are very or extremely active in avoiding ads, while another 40% are somewhat active.

The study concludes that ad avoidance is not defined by a specific demographic cohort, but rather represents a spectrum of active and passive behaviors. Consumers utilize ad-free services, ad blockers, skipping, muting, and scrolling past content to evade exposure. Omnicom's research examined conscious and unconscious responses, including attention and emotional reaction, to help clients understand how to make advertising feel more augmentative.

By identifying avoidance as a mindset rather than a fixed audience segment, brands can work on modifying consumer behavior through better execution. The findings aim to help clients design creative approaches—such as product placement, brand integration, and augmented reality—that consumers actively want to experience rather than tune out.

Why it mattersMarketers should treat ad avoidance as a mutable behavioral spectrum rather than writing off certain demographics. Creating non-disruptive, integrated advertising experiences like product placement and AR can help convert tuned-out audiences into leaned-in participants.

OpenAI Introduces Invisible Text Watermarking for ChatGPT in the EU

OpenAI has announced it will begin adding an invisible watermark to text generated by ChatGPT and Codex within the European Union. This rollout is designed to comply with the transparency mandates outlined in the EU AI Act, which took effect on August 2 and requires AI-generated content to be identifiable by other systems.

The watermark functions by subtly shaping the model's word choices to leave an imperceptible pattern that detectors can pick up, persisting even when text is copied and pasted. Developed alongside researchers from the University of Pennsylvania and Yale through a method called textGrain, the feature relies on a secret key to sort next-word predictions. However, OpenAI noted that the watermark can be disrupted by heavy editing, such as replacing 10% of words with synonyms, and is harder to detect in short passages or translations.

While the watermark is rolling out to EU users on all plans over the coming weeks and is available globally for developers using OpenAI's API, it is not being made a global default. OpenAI has restricted initial detector access to approved researchers and expert organizations to evaluate reliability and responsible uses.

Why it mattersMarketers must recognize that regulatory compliance for AI-generated content is tightening, particularly in Europe. As invisible watermarking becomes standard, brands should prepare for greater scrutiny over the authenticity of AI-assisted copywriting and content creation.

TechCrunch AIReflection AI

Reflection AI Debuts Beam Open-Weight Model to Challenge Chinese Rivals

Reflection AI has officially unveiled Beam, its first frontier open-weight AI model. The Brooklyn-based startup claims Beam matches the performance of leading Chinese open models on advanced reasoning benchmarks while operating at significantly lower compute costs, intensifying the competition to build Western alternatives to systems from providers like DeepSeek and Z.ai.

Beam is a 501-billion-parameter model featuring 23 billion active parameters, trained on 23.8 trillion tokens with a 1 million token context window. According to Reflection's internal benchmarks, the text-only mixture-of-experts model performs on par with Z.ai's GLM-5.2 and outperforms leading Western open models while utilizing three to four times less inference compute. The company is positioning Beam as a versatile workhorse for enterprises, the public sector, and developers.

Founded in 2024 by former Google DeepMind researchers, Reflection has raised roughly $4.7 billion from backers including Nvidia, Sequoia Capital, and Lightspeed Venture Partners at a $25 billion pre-money valuation. The startup has also secured substantial compute access through multi-billion-dollar deals with SpaceX and Nebius to fuel its infrastructure needs.

Why it mattersThe rapid advancement of cheaper, high-performance open-weight models gives enterprises and brands more cost-effective options for deploying custom AI agents. Marketers should monitor how open-source alternatives reduce the barrier to entry for proprietary brand applications.

AI Startup Instinct Expands Its Agent Into Group Chats

AI startup Instinct is expanding its capabilities by allowing users to add its AI agent to group chats, even with friends who do not have an Instinct account. The new feature is designed to simplify collaborative tasks like travel planning, organizing events, managing carpools, or coordinating group logistics within a single messaging thread.

To address privacy concerns, founder Noah Shinn explained that a user's personal Instinct agent must ask permission before connecting with a group's agent, and personal accounts remain strictly siloed from the group interaction. The feature is initially rolling out to early access users before a broader release, putting Instinct ahead of rivals like Meta's Muse in group-chat integration.

The update highlights an aggressive push among consumer AI developers to capture market share through collaboration tools. Competitors like OpenAI have similarly introduced personal agents and collaborative workspaces, signaling a shift toward multi-user AI utility.

Why it mattersAs AI agents infiltrate social group chats, brands face new frontiers for consumer engagement and community building. Marketers should consider how collaborative AI workflows will influence group purchasing decisions and consumer recommendations.

Zepto Capitalises on India's Growing Appetite for Korean Culture

As Gen Z and millennials in India turn their fascination with Korean entertainment into everyday lifestyle choices, the Hallyu wave is heavily influencing what consumers eat, buy, and apply. What began as a fondness for K-dramas and K-pop has transformed into a broader cultural movement driving demand across food, beauty, fashion, and tourism. On quick-commerce platform Zepto, this shift is clearly visible in shopping carts, with ramen searches crossing 4.6 lakh and Korean noodle searches surpassing 1 lakh.

Market data underscores the immense momentum of this trend, with the 2025 Overseas Hallyu Survey showing that 84.5% of Indian respondents view Korean pop culture favourably. Furthermore, India's K-beauty market is projected to reach $1.5 billion by 2030, according to research from Kindlife and Datum Intelligence. Quick-commerce has successfully shortened the distance between cultural discovery and actual purchase, enabling platforms like Zepto to integrate themselves directly into the consumer's lifestyle journey.

As entertainment rapidly translates into consumption, brands must recognize that Hallyu is no longer just content to watch, but a culture to participate in and buy into. Zepto's chief brand officer, Chandan Mendiratta, notes that the growth of Korean categories reflects a larger, ongoing cultural shift in Indian consumer habits.

Why it mattersMarketers must leverage pop-culture phenomena by utilizing quick-commerce to instantly bridge the gap between media exposure and consumer purchase. Brands should align their product offerings with lifestyle trends like K-culture to capture high-intent digital shoppers effectively.

Meesho Shares Jump Over 6% After Jefferies Retains 'Buy' Rating and Strong Growth Outlook

Shares of ecommerce company Meesho rose 6.1% to an intraday high of ₹240.40 on the BSE after Jefferies maintained its 'Buy' rating and set a target price of ₹240. The rally pushed Meesho's market capitalisation to approximately ₹1.10 Lakh Cr ($11.5 Bn). Jefferies highlighted management's projection of a 25% CAGR in net merchandise value (NMV) over the next five years, fueled by user growth, higher order frequency, and logistics efficiencies. Additional growth drivers include improving contribution margins, financial services opportunities, and scaling higher-value categories.

In regulatory filings, Meesho reported explosive growth in its content commerce segment, with NMV surging 152% year-on-year in the 12 months leading up to August 2026. This was powered by 1.6 lakh active creators, of which nano creators with under 10,000 followers accounted for 90%. Non-metro areas made up 81% of active creators, and Tier III and IV cities drove 66% of content commerce orders. Women's fashion remained the top category, while the short-form video platform Video Finds garnered nearly 4,800 crore views. Concurrently, Meesho's consolidated net loss narrowed significantly by 54.1% to ₹132.8 Cr in Q1 FY27, alongside a 48% rise in marketplace revenue.

Driven by its value-led positioning and extensive reach beyond metro cities, Meesho continues to strengthen its market standing. The company is also considered a high-conviction candidate for inclusion in the MSCI India Global Standard Index during its upcoming November review.

Why it mattersBrands operating in non-metro markets should invest heavily in nano-creator networks and short-form video commerce to drive high engagement and conversions. Tapping into regional consumer bases through localized, trusted content creators can unlock explosive, cost-effective growth.

Premium Staples Brands Face Growth and Customer Retention Challenges

A growing crop of Indian brands is asking households to reconsider everyday staples like atta, oil, ghee, and salt by offering alternatives made differently, such as freshly milled flour, cold-pressed oils, and traditionally made ghee. Companies operating in this space include Anveshan, Barosi, Gramiyaa, Two Brothers Organic Farms, Khetika, Akshayakalpa Organic, Sid’s Farm, and KisaanSay. While these brands have found initial buyers through quick commerce and modern trade channels, the biggest challenge lies in bridging the gap between consumers trying a sample and becoming loyal, repeat buyers who do not tinker with their kitchen basics.

Despite these habit-formation hurdles, the sector is backed by substantial capital. Disclosed venture and debt funding across Zoff, KisaanSay, Anveshan, Anmasa, Sid’s Farm, Doodhvale Farms, and Indic Wisdom amounted to roughly ₹335 Cr in funding rounds reported in 2026, alongside undisclosed raises by Rosier Foods and Khetika. This capital is primarily directed toward manufacturing, procurement, testing, and distribution to build the capacity to deliver consistent quality at scale. Brands are also aiming for aggressive revenue targets, with Anveshan projecting a jump from an annual run rate of ₹280–₹300 Cr to over ₹1,000 Cr, and Khetika targeting ₹2,000 Cr over the next two to three years following ₹247 Cr in FY25 revenue, while legacy giants like Tata Sampann continue expanding their presence.

Why it mattersFor performance and brand marketers, transitioning consumers from trial sampling to habitual, long-term purchases in low-involvement categories like staples requires clear, trusted proof of health and quality. Brands must invest heavily in supply chain reliability and distribution infrastructure to justify higher price points against conventional grocery options.

X Launches X Numbers Feature Amid Scrutiny on Messaging Platforms

X has launched a new feature called X Numbers, which allows users to send messages or make calls without needing to share their phone numbers. This rollout comes at a time when regulatory scrutiny on communication privacy features is high in India.

Previously, the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology (MeitY) blocked similar username-based features on competing platforms like WhatsApp, Signal, and Telegram over concerns related to fraud risks. The introduction of X Numbers raises questions about how regulators will approach similar functionalities on X moving forward.

Why it mattersMarketers relying on messaging platforms must monitor regulatory shifts closely, as compliance crackdowns on user privacy features can suddenly disrupt communication channels. Brands should prepare for stricter government oversight regarding how digital platforms handle user contact data and fraud prevention.
