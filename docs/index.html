FSSAI seizes food products worth ₹82 crore and cancels 52 licences in festive crackdown

The Food Safety and Standards Authority of India (FSSAI) has seized unsafe food products valued at ₹82 crore during a major enforcement crackdown ahead of the festive season. Alongside the substantial product seizures, regulatory authorities have cancelled 52 business licences for non-compliance.

The nationwide operation targeted adulterated and substandard food items commonly in high demand during festival periods. FSSAI's intensified monitoring aims to protect consumer health and weed out fraudulent operators capitalizing on festive demand surges.

Food businesses and FMCG brands must tighten their internal quality controls and supply chain auditing to prevent severe regulatory penalties, licence cancellations, and public reputational damage during peak sales windows.

Why it mattersBrands must enforce rigorous quality control across their supply chains during high-volume periods to avoid devastating regulatory penalties. Protecting consumer trust through transparent compliance is critical, especially when festive scrutiny peaks.

CFOs face mounting costs, consent management challenges, and supply chain risks as DPDP deadline approaches

Chief Financial Officers (CFOs) are currently grappling with significant financial and operational hurdles as the compliance deadline for the Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act draws nearer. Organizations are struggling with the direct costs of implementation, complex consent management requirements, and underlying supply chain vulnerabilities.

The regulatory pressure forces companies to overhaul how they collect, store, and process consumer data. Businesses must allocate substantial budgets towards technical infrastructure and legal frameworks to meet the stringent data privacy mandates.

As the deadline approaches, finance and marketing leaders must collaborate closely to audit third-party vendors and restructure data collection mechanisms. Failing to comply risks hefty financial penalties and long-term erosion of consumer trust.

Why it mattersMarketers must align data collection and consent strategies with strict privacy regulations to mitigate financial and legal risks. Building robust data governance directly impacts consumer trust and ensures continuity across third-party supply chains.

South Korea moves to crack down on online shopping 'dark patterns'

South Korea is taking regulatory action to crack down on deceptive online shopping practices known as "dark patterns." These user interface designs trick consumers into making unintended choices, such as recurring subscriptions or accidental purchases.

The regulatory crackdown targets digital manipulation tactics that distort consumer intent on e-commerce platforms. Authorities are tightening oversight to protect online shoppers from manipulative design techniques commonly deployed by digital retailers.

E-commerce brands and digital marketers operating in regulated markets must review their UX/UI design choices to eliminate deceptive practices. Transparent user journeys are now vital to avoid regulatory crackdowns and build sustainable brand loyalty.

Why it mattersDigital marketers must phase out deceptive UX tactics like dark patterns to stay ahead of tightening global regulations. Embracing transparent design protects brand equity and fosters long-term customer relationships built on trust.
