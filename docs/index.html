Google Launches Gemini 4 Argon With 1 Million-Token Output Limit

Google has unveiled Gemini 4 Argon, its most powerful AI model to date, aimed at tackling long-running workloads across enterprise knowledge work, software engineering, and cybersecurity. The tech giant is initially rolling out Argon to select participants through its Fairwind programme, with priority access slated for Google AI Ultra subscribers and paid API customers ahead of an unannounced broader public release.

A standout technical shift in Gemini 4 Argon is its 1 million-token output limit, significantly raised from the previous 64K-token ceiling. Google noted this expanded capacity enables the model to sustain longer reasoning trajectories and complete complex, multi-step workflows in a single run. The model will launch at an introductory price of $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens, with cached input tokens discounted by 95%.

Beyond coding—where it scored 77.9% on the DeepSWE v1.1 benchmark—Argon is built for knowledge-intensive enterprise tasks such as drafting, legal research, and finance. DeepMind’s testing indicates high performance in long-context scenarios and scientific problem-solving. Additionally, the model offers multimodal features capable of analyzing charts, processing long videos, and cross-referencing multiple documents at once.

Why it mattersThe leap to a 1 million-token output limit enables enterprise marketing teams to analyze massive datasets, long-form assets, and extensive video documentation in single-run workflows. With steep discounts on cached inputs, deploying advanced multimodal reasoning models into ongoing martech operations will become considerably more cost-efficient.

SBI Mutual Fund Raises Stake in Swiggy to Over 5% Following Open Market Purchase

SBI Mutual Fund has acquired an additional 1.18 Cr equity shares of foodtech major Swiggy through open market transactions. Based on the closing price of ₹253.90 on the day of the trade, the fund house invested over ₹300.6 Cr, lifting its total shareholding past the mandatory 5% disclosure threshold to approximately 5.1%. This transaction comes shortly after Swiggy secured shareholder approval to cap foreign ownership at 49.5%, earning it the status of an Indian-owned and controlled company. This structural shift allows Swiggy to pivot its quick commerce arm, Instamart, toward an inventory-led model to directly procure and sell products, a move brokerage Jefferies estimates could drive an 80 bps margin upside.

Financially, Swiggy reported a narrowed net loss of ₹791 Cr in Q1 FY27, marking a 34% year-on-year improvement, while operating revenue surged 36.8% to ₹6,812 Cr. Instamart also achieved a key profitability milestone by reaching contribution margin breakeven during the quarter. Despite these operational advancements, domestic mutual funds collectively held a 22.8% stake in Swiggy as of June 30, 2026, compared to rival Eternal's 31.7%. Swiggy shares closed 4.88% lower at ₹238 on the BSE following the market updates.

Why it mattersMarketers working with quick-commerce platforms should watch the shift toward inventory-led models, which allow for deeper brand partnerships and direct procurement. As platforms like Instamart restructure for better margins, brand visibility and inventory placement strategies will need to adapt accordingly.

Stakeholders Warn TRAI That 5G Network Slicing Must Not Compromise Net Neutrality

During a recent TRAI open house discussion, industry stakeholders including the Broadband India Forum (BIF), consumer groups, Bharti Airtel, and Reliance Jio presented their perspectives on the proposed regulatory framework for 5G network slicing and quality of service. A core concern raised during the session was ensuring that advanced network slicing capabilities do not dilute core net neutrality principles.

The consultation highlights the delicate regulatory balance telecom authorities must strike as 5G services expand across India. Network slicing allows operators to partition networks for specialized services, but consumer advocates and industry players are pushing for safeguards to prevent discriminatory access or degraded speeds for standard traffic.

TRAI will now review the inputs from carriers, industry bodies, and consumer advocates to shape the final policy. The resulting framework will dictate how telecom operators deploy 5G features while maintaining open internet standards.

Why it mattersBrand and performance marketers relying on seamless, high-speed mobile delivery must monitor how TRAI balances network slicing with net neutrality. Any shifts in telecom quality of service frameworks could directly impact mobile-first ad delivery, video streaming experiences, and consumer engagement.
