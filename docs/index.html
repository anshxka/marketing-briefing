🤖AI in India & global

Inc42MeitY

MeitY Proposes DEPA Framework to Unlock India-Specific Data for Training AI Models

The Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology (MeitY) has introduced a consent-based framework to enable secure data sharing for training artificial intelligence models across India. Dubbed the Data Empowerment and Protection Architecture (DEPA) for AI training, the initiative aims to help startups, businesses, and academic researchers access large-scale, India-specific datasets while ensuring privacy and compliance with the Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act.

Formulated in collaboration with think tank iSPIRT, the techno-legal architecture allows organisations to collaborate on AI model training without directly transferring underlying datasets. The system is built on four core technical pillars: Confidential Clean Rooms for secure computing, Differential Privacy to ensure individual-level details cannot be extracted, Electronic Contracts to enforce machine-readable terms, and Zero-Trust Security featuring continuous authentication and encryption.

The framework defines three primary stakeholder groups: Training Data Providers such as banks, hospitals, and government agencies; Training Data Consumers including startups and enterprises; and Data Collectives that aggregate, standardise, and govern data pools. Under DEPA, data providers will anonymise and encrypt their datasets before registering them with metadata on designated data exchange platforms.

Why it mattersThis framework signals that Indian marketers may soon gain compliant access to vast domestic datasets to build more accurate, culturally nuanced predictive and generative AI models. As clean rooms and differential privacy become institutionalised, brands will need to adapt their own data strategies to collaborate securely within government-backed data exchanges.

MediaNamaShadowfax

Shadowfax Q2 Revenue Climbs 57% to Rs 1,538 Crore as Profit Jumps Fivefold

Logistics and last-mile delivery provider Shadowfax posted a 57% year-on-year increase in operating revenue to Rs 1,537.7 crore for Q2 FY27, up from Rs 982.1 crore in the corresponding period last year. Net profit for the quarter rose more than fivefold to Rs 68 crore from Rs 13 crore a year earlier, as revenue growth outpaced a 54% rise in total expenses, which reached Rs 1,490.2 crore.

Express parcel services remained the company's core contributor, generating Rs 1,096 crore or 71% of total revenue on 21.7 crore orders. Meanwhile, its hyperlocal commerce arm, which powers fulfillment for food delivery and quick commerce platforms, grew 88% year-on-year to Rs 341 crore, accounting for roughly 22% of revenue and handling 5.4 crore orders. Across all verticals, Shadowfax fulfilled 27.1 crore deliveries during the quarter.

The company stated it is expanding faster than the blended quick commerce sector, aided by zero-commission low-value food delivery setups such as Swiggy's Toing and Rapido's Ownly. To support D2C brands, Shadowfax is launching micro-fulfilment centers, leaning on its network as a third-party logistics provider offering same-day delivery across more than 120 cities.

Why it mattersThe rapid scale of 3PL micro-fulfilment networks allows D2C brands to offer same-day delivery without building expensive captive logistics. Marketers can now leverage this third-party infrastructure to compete on speed with major e-commerce and quick commerce platforms across 120+ cities.
