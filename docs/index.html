TechCrunch AIMeta

Meta Launches Muse Wonder, Its First Frontier AI Model Built with Active Memory

Meta’s Superintelligence Lab has officially rolled out its first frontier AI models, Muse Wonder and Muse Wonder Full. Moving beyond the lightweight, low-latency focus of its earlier Muse Spark release, these larger models are designed to tackle complex tasks such as coding, posting benchmark results that rival Fable 5.1 and GPT-6 Astra. The models were trained at Meta’s Prometheus data center in central Ohio and mark the team's first frontier launch since Scale AI founder Alexandr Wang reorganized Meta’s AI initiatives in August of last year.

A key focus for Meta is long-term retention through an attribute termed "active memory," aimed at powering personal agents capable of working over extended time horizons. Meta reports that Muse Wonder outperformed Fable, Opus, and Astra on the continual-learning benchmark MemoryBench, with researchers emphasizing its capability to retain and synthesize user information across days, weeks, and months.

Muse Wonder is priced for commercial use through the Meta Model API at $4 per million input tokens and $20 per million output tokens, comparable to GPT-6 Sol and Opus 5.5. Meanwhile, Muse Wonder Full will be reserved exclusively for Muse Code subscribers, and both variants will serve as the underlying foundation for the Muse agent.

Why it mattersThe shift toward models with long-term 'active memory' unlocks possibilities for persistent brand agents that can track customer preferences over weeks and months rather than single sessions. However, the premium API pricing means performance marketers must carefully evaluate whether frontier-grade capabilities offer enough ROI over lightweight models for routine conversational use cases.

ET BrandEquityConsumer behaviour & trends

Social Algorithms and Venue Inflation Shift Navratri Consumer Spending: Study

The nine-night festival of Navratri has transformed into a commercialised retail event driven by social media algorithms, venue price inflation, and fasting food supply anxieties, according to a joint study by strategy consultancy Langoor and analytics firm Quilt.AI. Tracking consumer search queries and online conversations across Delhi, Mumbai, Bangalore, and Hyderabad, the report reveals that consumer interest is split between sacred rituals (55 per cent) and commercial spectacle including social events, dance, and fashion (46 per cent).

A primary commercial catalyst is the viral "nine-night wardrobe algorithm," where digital platforms prescribe specific attire shades for each evening. Search queries for these daily colour sequences rise into the tens of thousands monthly ahead of the festival, converting single purchases into multi-day wardrobe planning. In Hyderabad, search demand for verified colour lists reached 99.9 per cent, prompting retailers such as Senco, Mavuris, and Aza to launch daily colour-themed drops and accessory bundles.

Simultaneously, soaring ticket prices at commercial garba dance venues—climbing beyond Rs 2,00,000 in Mumbai and driving organizers to offer installment plans—are pushing consumers toward local community celebrations. Coupled with growing consumer anxiety over supply chain reliability for fasting foods, festive spending is increasingly balancing hyper-curated fashion demands with budget-conscious local festivities.

Why it mattersCultural moments are increasingly gamified by social media narratives, unlocking multi-day purchase occasions for fashion and lifestyle categories. However, as venue inflation prices out mass audiences, brands must balance premium positioning with accessible community-led engagement.
