The Food Safety and Standards Authority of India (FSSAI) has widened its regulatory crackdown, issuing fresh show-cause notices to quick commerce platform Zepto alongside several food and nutraceutical brands. DigidayApple

Apple Explains Safari Crackdown on Email-Based Identity Tracking Solutions

Apple has published a blog post outlining its rationale for cutting off ad-tech companies that track web users across its Safari browser. The crackdown targets identity solutions that capture email addresses during logins or newsletter sign-ups and convert them into universal identifiers. These alternative IDs allow advertisers to recognize and profile individuals across different websites without relying on traditional third-party cookies.

The Safari block specifically affects identity and ad-tech firms such as LiveRamp, ID5, Permutive, Audigent, and The Trade Desk's Unified ID 2.0 (UID 2.0). Apple confirmed the blocking mechanism applies across iPhones, iPads, Macs, and Vision Pro devices. When users update their software, Safari ceases communications with targeted domains, effectively shutting down cross-site tracking.

Apple argued that email addresses have effectively replaced cookies as the primary mechanism for persistent web surveillance. Because individuals often keep a single email address for years across all personal devices, identity graphs built from these addresses create far more durable profiles than legacy cookies. Apple noted that unlinking or unsubscribing fails to remove retained tracking codes, prompting the company to implement an outright block on the entire web addresses of targeted vendors.

Why it mattersPerformance marketers relying on alternative identity solutions like UID 2.0 to replace third-party cookies must prepare for severe signal loss across Apple devices. Brands should accelerate investments in transparent, privacy-first first-party data strategies rather than relying on workarounds that platform gatekeepers will inevitably block.
