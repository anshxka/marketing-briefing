ET BrandEquityNasscom

Only 13% of Indian Enterprises Have Scaled Voice AI Deployments, Nasscom Reports

Voice AI adoption in India is moving beyond experimentation, but scaled enterprise deployment remains limited. According to Nasscom's 'India Voice AI Readiness 2026' report, developed with Krisp, only 13% of organizations have deployed Voice AI across multiple customer journeys. More than half remain in early phases, with 23% exploring the technology and 32% running proof-of-concept projects. Another 31% have reached limited production, and only 9% use Voice AI to handle more than half of their eligible customer interactions.

The survey of 140 organizations—including BPM providers, GCCs, and enterprises—indicates that operational efficiency is the primary driver of adoption. Among respondents, 61% reported a reduction of more than 20% in cost per eligible interaction, and nearly two-thirds cited a 10% to 30% reduction in average handling time. Furthermore, 68% of organizations realize returns on their Voice AI investments within six to 24 months.

Despite these benefits, hurdles around multilingual accuracy, legacy systems, and enterprise workflow integration continue to limit scale. Measurement also remains narrow: 45% of respondents evaluate impact only for select deployments, and just 14% track broader business outcomes comprehensively. The report advises organizations to shift evaluation metrics away from mere call volume and handling time toward resolution success, customer satisfaction, human escalation rates, and cost per completed task.

Why it mattersMarketers relying on automated customer touchpoints must focus on resolution quality and multilingual capabilities rather than simple call deflection. Ensuring Voice AI resolves tasks effectively is vital to protecting customer satisfaction and brand trust at scale.

TechCrunch AIMicrosoft

Satya Nadella Calls for an 'Emergency Brake' and Stricter AI Trust Architecture

Microsoft Chief Executive Officer Satya Nadella has called for an overhaul of artificial intelligence safety standards, urging the industry to build an operational "emergency brake" into models. In a post on X, Nadella stated that it is time to step back and critically evaluate the trust architecture governing AI systems.

Nadella warned that the tech sector cannot treat what he termed "Super Intelligence" as nested black boxes while passively accepting or rejecting their recommendations and actions. Instead, he proposed separating the core model from the harness that orchestrates its execution, alongside externalizing controls and safeguards.

Under Nadella's framework, every meaningful action taken by a model must generate tamper-proof, human-readable evidence. He asserted that systems must assume an AI model is compromised from the start, requiring authorized personnel to retain the capability to pause or completely shut down an active system mid-task.

The commentary comes amid a rising number of disclosed incidents where prominent AI firms appeared to lose control of their models, and follows Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei's recent proposals for more cautious AI development.

Why it mattersNadella's push highlights that enterprise AI deployment requires strict human-in-the-loop controls rather than blind automation across customer-facing campaigns and data workflows. Marketers integrating generative AI must ensure their tech stacks feature audit trails and quick override mechanisms to safeguard brand trust.
