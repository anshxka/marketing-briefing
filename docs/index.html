Amazon Ads introduces long-term metrics to capture value beyond attribution windows

Amazon is pushing to transform ad measurement by arguing that a significant portion of a brand's value on its platform materializes well after traditional attribution windows close. By studying over 2 million campaigns in June, Amazon estimated that roughly 20 percent of a brand's sales value shows up over time outside the standard seven-to-30-day window. To address this, Amazon unveiled a long-term sales metric in closed beta back in October 2024. This tool tracks shoppers new to a brand and uses historical 12-month return data to estimate future campaign generation.

Building on this initiative, Amazon announced full-funnel campaigns last November to shift budgets across various ad products and drive long-term sales. Furthermore, the company plans to extend look-back windows in its clean room this spring, allowing marketers to verify whether projected long-term sales actually materialized. These measurement updates aim to capture actions such as branded searches and filled carts that eventually convert within 12 months.

Brand advertisers stand to gain the most from these nuanced measurement capabilities, as their upper-funnel spending has historically been difficult to justify. As platforms like Amazon, Google, and social channels compete for ad dollars amid shifting consumer behaviors, proving long-term value is becoming essential for brands.

Why it mattersFor performance and brand marketers, Amazon's push for long-term attribution means a better ability to justify upper-funnel investments that do not convert immediately. Brands can now leverage these extended metrics to capture the true lifetime value of customers acquired through marketplace advertising.

Measurement gaps and data concerns keep ChatGPT advertising budgets at test levels

Seven months after OpenAI began selling ads in ChatGPT, widespread measurement gaps and tracking discrepancies are keeping brand budgets at a purely test level. Agencies report unusual hurdles compared to established giants like Google and Meta, including delayed conversion reporting, click discrepancies, and a lack of demographic or prompt data. For instance, U.K. lead-gen campaigns run by Accuracast showed zero conversions weeks later, alongside mismatched click counts that OpenAI's support team could not explain.

Beyond technical hurdles, many brand legal teams are hesitant to sign OpenAI's tracking terms due to liability concerns and unease over customer data usage. Unlike Google and Meta, whose terms advertisers have trusted for years, OpenAI reportedly offers little room for negotiation or red lines. These combined data and measurement anxieties are also slowing the adoption of custom audiences on the platform.

While some agencies report smooth execution without click discrepancies, the overall friction highlights the growing pains of a new ad seller. Advertisers remain cautious until robust reporting and trusted data-sharing frameworks are firmly established.

Why it mattersMarketers should note that while conversational AI platforms represent exciting new frontiers, foundational measurement and data trust must precede large budget allocations. Verifying attribution and navigating legal data terms carefully will prevent wasted spend on emerging channels.

SBI Mutual Fund increases stake in Swiggy to over 5% following open market purchases worth ₹300 Cr

SBI Mutual Fund acquired an additional 1.18 million equity shares of foodtech major Swiggy via open market transactions, pushing its total shareholding to 14.16 million equity shares or approximately 5.1% of the company's total equity. Based on the closing price of ₹253.90 on the day of the trade, the mutual fund spent over ₹300.6 crore to raise its stake. This transaction lifts SBI Mutual Fund's aggregate holding past the mandatory 5% disclosure threshold under SEBI regulations, moving up from its previous 4.7% stake.

The investment coincides with Swiggy receiving shareholder approval to cap foreign ownership at 49.5%, securing the status of an Indian-owned and controlled company. This classification enables Swiggy to pivot its quick-commerce arm, Instamart, toward an inventory-led model to procure products directly from brands and lift revenues and margins. Financially, Swiggy reported a nearly 34% year-on-year reduction in net loss to ₹791 crore for Q1 FY27, alongside a 36.8% increase in operating revenue to ₹6,812 crore, while Instamart achieved contribution margin breakeven.

The company will likely focus on executing the inventory-led model for Instamart to capture anticipated margin upsides. Domestic mutual funds currently hold a cumulative 22.8% stake in Swiggy, and further institutional activity will depend on the quick commerce unit's operational scaling and path to sustained profitability.

Why it mattersMarketers should monitor Instamart's shift to a 1P inventory-led model, which will change brand negotiation and direct-procurement strategies. Institutional backing from major funds like SBI signals continued confidence in quick-commerce unit economics despite broader market volatility.

Industry stakeholders recently gathered for a TRAI open house discussion to share their perspectives on the proposed framework for 5G network slicing and quality of service. Participants included the Broadband India Forum (BIF), various consumer groups, and major telecom operators such as Airtel and Jio.

A central theme of the discussion was ensuring that the implementation of 5G network slicing does not compromise or dilute the core principles of net neutrality. The regulatory body will review these stakeholder submissions to shape the final policy framework governing 5G services in India, determining how operators can commercially deploy network slices while maintaining fair access.
