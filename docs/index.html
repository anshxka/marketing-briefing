Today's big picture

  1. The boundary between media consumption and instant fulfillment is disappearing in India, evidenced by JioHotstar and Instamart's StreamToCart partnership, even as quick commerce matures into a channel driven predominantly by existing hero SKUs rather than experimental launches.
  2. Substantial capital flows into Indian AI startups and platform tools—from Google's campaign translation to Mobavenue's retargeting—signal that generative and automated tech is moving from experimental novelty to core performance infrastructure, raising the stakes for execution accuracy.
  3. Legacy brands like Safi and staples like Kurkure are actively decentralizing their traditional mass-media playbooks, pivoting toward digital-first Gen Z acquisition and hyper-regional product localization to capture pockets of resilient rural and youth-driven growth.

💡 Tip of the day

DigidayU.S. Small Business Administration

U.S. Small Business Administration Formally Classifies Content Creators as Small Businesses

On Oct. 15, the U.S. Small Business Administration (SBA) issued the federal government's first small business classification guidance specifically for content creators. Creator-led businesses are now formally grouped under the SBA's Motion Picture and Video industries category, clarifying which North American Industry Classification System (NAICS) codes they should apply under.

While creators were technically eligible for SBA backing previously by meeting core criteria around size, credit availability, and location, the lack of a defined classification created operational roadblocks. The update follows months of proposed amendments aimed at systematically integrating millions of creator enterprises into the federal support ecosystem.

Industry observers consider the formal classification a crucial milestone for legitimizing the creator economy. Daniel Abas, founder and president of the Creators Guild of America, pointed out that creators have shaped commerce and culture for over a decade yet were historically treated as short-term marketing expenses rather than formal enterprises.

The shift is poised to alter how corporate stakeholders manage creator deals. Lauren Lyster, VP and head of social media at Go Fish Digital, noted that formal government recognition encourages banks, CFOs, and procurement departments to view creators as an asset class, likely shifting brand compensation structures from ad media buys into standard supplier contracts.

Why it mattersBrand marketers and agencies will face tighter scrutiny from procurement and finance teams as creator engagements shift from media buys into formal vendor management. Treating creator-led businesses as legitimate suppliers requires more rigorous contracting, compliance, and long-term partnership strategies.

Inc42Jio Platforms

Jio Platforms Surges 21% on Market Debut Following India's Largest IPO

Jio Platforms made a strong stock market debut, listing at ₹1,358 per share on the BSE and NSE—a 21.36% premium over its issue price of ₹1,119. The listing catapulted the market capitalisation of Reliance Industries' telecom and digital arm to ₹12.5 Lakh Cr (around $131 Bn), compared to ₹10.3 Lakh Cr at the issue price. The public offering, which was India's largest ever at ₹30,213 Cr, consisted entirely of a fresh issue of 27 Cr equity shares and was subscribed 22.19 times by the close of bidding on October 23. Ahead of the issue, the company secured ₹8,109.8 Cr from marquee anchor investors, including BlackRock, SBI Mutual Fund, HDFC Mutual Fund, ICICI Prudential Mutual Fund, ADIA, and the Government of Singapore.

The company has earmarked ₹27,500 Cr of the IPO proceeds to prepay borrowings of its telecom subsidiary, Reliance Jio Infocomm (RJIL), with the remainder set aside for general corporate purposes. While the capital primarily strengthens the balance sheet, Jio Platforms' future growth strategy relies heavily on diversifying beyond mobile connectivity into cloud services, fixed broadband, artificial intelligence, and enterprise solutions.

Operationally, RJIL serviced 533.3 Mn subscribers as of June 30, 2026, carrying approximately 60% of India’s wireless data traffic in FY26. Average revenue per user (ARPU) improved by 3.26% year-on-year to ₹215.60, alongside a surge in monthly data consumption to 43.7 GB per user from 37 GB. Simultaneously, its fixed broadband base reached 28.6 Mn—bolstered by 14 Mn JioAirFiber connections—helping drive full-year operating revenue up nearly 15% to ₹1.47 Lakh Cr and net profit to ₹30,049 Cr. Following the debut, Motilal Oswal initiated coverage on Jio Platforms with a 'BUY' rating and a target price of ₹1,350.

Why it mattersJio's deepening household penetration and swelling data consumption offer brand marketers an unprecedented, unified digital highway to target Indian consumers at scale. The company's expansion into connected homes and enterprise ecosystems signals more integrated ad formats and touchpoints across the consumer journey.
