TechCrunch AIOpenAI

OpenAI Launches ChatGPT Wallet to Let AI Agents Make Direct Purchases

OpenAI has introduced a new wallet feature for ChatGPT that allows its AI agents, known as dots, to help users choose products and complete purchases on their behalf. The functionality is rolling out initially to a select group of U.S. ChatGPT Pro subscribers aged 18 and older who hold a U.S.-issued Visa, Mastercard, or American Express card.

Shoppers maintain control over purchases by reviewing order selections, total prices, and chosen payment methods before checkout, finalizing each transaction via biometric authentication like Face ID or fingerprint scans. To preserve security, the system utilizes tokenized card details so sensitive payment information is never exposed to the agent or merchant. Merchants automatically receive necessary fulfillment data such as shipping addresses, while consumers retain standard credit card reward earnings and dispute protections.

The move comes as tech platforms test the viability of agentic commerce. While services like Uber and DoorDash have documented limited volumes from AI purchasing agents, Meta's Muse platform—which currently counts over 10 million users—has seen AI-guided shopping emerge as a core user activity. Competing personal agents such as Instinct have also enabled automated checkouts.

OpenAI plans to use this limited testing phase to evaluate the system before rolling it out more widely. Future iterations are expected to introduce expanded buyer controls, including the ability to set specific spending limits, timeframe parameters, and restricted purchase categories.

Why it mattersThe transition of AI from advisory chatbots to autonomous purchasing agents will dramatically compress the traditional digital marketing funnel. Brands must ensure product feeds, pricing, and merchant data are optimized for agent discovery and checkout, as algorithms increasingly become the end buyer.

SoftBank sells Meesho shares worth ₹1,885 crore in bulk block deal

Japanese investment giant SoftBank has offloaded 7.6 crore shares of listed e-commerce major Meesho through a block deal valued at ₹1,885.2 crore. Conducted via its affiliate SVF II Meerkat (DE) LLC on the NSE, the shares were sold at ₹246.75 apiece, reflecting a 4% discount to the stock’s previous closing price. The sell-off represents nearly a quarter of SoftBank's Meesho holdings, following an earlier sale of 8 crore shares for ₹1,650.4 crore in September. Prior to this sale, SoftBank held a 6.84% equity stake comprising 31.6 crore shares.

Institutional investors swiftly absorbed the supply. Fidelity emerged as the largest buyer, acquiring shares worth ₹573.1 crore across multiple funds, alongside Societe Generale (₹364.6 crore) and Morgan Stanley (₹244.9 crore). Domestic asset managers also participated actively, with Motilal Oswal Mutual Fund purchasing stakes worth ₹129.5 crore and Aditya Birla Sun Life Mutual Fund buying ₹103.4 crore, with Goldman Sachs among other buyers.

The profit-taking comes amid an upswing in Meesho’s stock, which has climbed 32.8% over the past three months and 42.6% year-to-date. The rally has been supported by strengthening financial fundamentals: in Q2 FY27, Meesho reported a 34% year-on-year increase in revenue to ₹4,131.6 crore and narrowed its net loss by 68% to ₹132.5 crore, up from ₹411.4 crore in Q2 FY26.

Why it mattersMeesho's steady march toward profitability and strong revenue growth show that value-conscious, Tier 2+ e-commerce can yield viable operating margins. The robust institutional absorption of SoftBank's stake underscores growing market confidence in Meesho's long-term commercial model.

Razorpay Secures SEBI Approval for ₹6,000 Crore Domestic IPO

Fintech major Razorpay has received approval from the Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) to proceed with its proposed initial public offering of ₹5,000 Cr to ₹6,000 Cr ($530 Mn to $630 Mn). The market regulator issued its observations on November 3 following a confidential draft filing submitted in June. The public listing is anticipated to value the company at around $5 Bn (₹47,000 Cr), with the market debut expected to take place between January and March 2027.

Founded in 2014 by Shashank Kumar and Harshil Mathur, Razorpay provides digital payment processing, business banking, payroll, and lending services. Ahead of the planned domestic listing, which will consist of both a fresh issue and an offer-for-sale, the company shifted its domicile from the United States to India. In FY25, its operating revenue grew 65% to ₹3,783 Cr, though one-time tax and restructuring expenses from redomiciling, along with ₹1,209 Cr in ESOP costs, pushed it into a net loss.

Alongside its listing preparations, Razorpay has been building out its artificial intelligence and growth marketing capabilities. The fintech recently expanded its partnership with OpenAI to enable domestic brands to manage and launch ad campaigns on ChatGPT Ads via its Razorpay Engage platform. It has also introduced Vulcan, an in-house transformer-based AI foundation model designed to optimize payment routing and identify digital transaction fraud in real time.

Why it mattersRazorpay's expansion into AI-powered advertising solutions like ChatGPT Ads allows marketers to bridge performance marketing and checkout infrastructure on a unified platform. A successful public listing will further validate the enterprise software ecosystem supporting India's direct-to-consumer and digital commerce brands.

Former Indian cricketer Sachin Tendulkar has acquired an equity stake in Hyderabad-based cleantech startup Recykal for an undisclosed amount. The investment was made during an extension of the startup's recently concluded $23 Mn bridge round, which previously closed a $17.6 Mn equity infusion in June alongside a $5.4 Mn secondary transaction. The startup has raised over $60 Mn to date from investors including Morgan Stanley, 360 ONE, Circulate Capital, and the Murugappa Group.

Founded in 2016 by Abhay Deshpande, Abhishek Deshpande, Ekta Narain, Vikram Prabakar, and Anirudha Jalan, Recykal operates a B2B marketplace for recyclable materials and provides compliance software to help companies track plastic and e-waste. The company's network includes more than 5,000 waste aggregators and recyclers, catering to 650 enterprise clients such as Hindustan Unilever, Samsung, and BMW.

Recykal has also rolled out a consumer-facing deposit return system (DRS) using reverse vending machines, where consumers pay a refundable deposit at purchase and recover it upon returning used packaging. The startup reported gross revenue of ₹1,498 Cr in FY26, up 53.2% from ₹978 Cr in the prior year, while net losses expanded 7% to ₹60 Cr.

Why it mattersWith enterprise brands under mounting regulatory pressure to document plastic and packaging compliance, tech-led waste tracking is becoming core to corporate reputation and operations.
