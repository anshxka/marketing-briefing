Urban Company Board Approves ₹2,500 Crore Fundraise as Losses Widen

The board of listed home services major Urban Company has approved a proposal to raise up to ₹2,500 crore to bolster its war chest. The planned capital infusion comes at a crucial juncture as the company faces aggressive competition in its instant home services arm, InstaHelp, from rivals Snabbit and Pronto, while simultaneously seeking to expand its product footprint under its Native brand into the residential air conditioner space.

The company’s financials reflect the rising cost of these initiatives. For Q2 FY27, Urban Company reported an adjusted EBITDA loss of ₹144 crore, heavily influenced by a ₹174 crore investment into InstaHelp; excluding InstaHelp, the core business posted an adjusted EBITDA profit of ₹30 crore. Consolidated revenue from operations increased 41.4% year-on-year to ₹537.3 crore, but total expenses jumped 61.5% to ₹745.7 crore, causing net losses to more than triple to ₹182.5 crore from ₹59.3 crore a year prior.

Management rejected claims that the fundraise is merely a buffer for InstaHelp's burn, stating the company can hit profitability targets without it. Instead, CEO Abhiraj Singh Bhal outlined ambitions to transform Urban Company into the "operating system for the Indian home." The capital will provide strategic flexibility for domestic and international growth, including potential acquisitions to consolidate current markets or enter new categories, though acquisitions within InstaHelp remain highly unlikely.

Why it mattersUrban Company's strategy highlights how established service platforms are forced to heavily fund defensive quick-service plays while venturing into hardware to secure brand stickiness. For marketers, transitioning from an on-demand utility to an overarching home ecosystem requires balancing high customer-acquisition spends with the sustained trust needed to sell durable goods.

ET BrandEquityCase studies, jobs & competitor intel

AI Assistants Shift Discovery from Search Rank to Upstream Brand Judgment

The rapid adoption of AI assistants is fundamentally changing brand discovery by transforming open search into automated judgment. In traditional search, search engines returned links and allowed users to evaluate options. In contrast, AI assistants process user prompts—such as budget-conscious vacation planning—and return direct recommendations with reasoning attached, leaving non-recommended competitors entirely omitted from the conversation.

This shift means simply being indexed or cited is no longer sufficient. An AI assistant may reference a brand for care guidelines on a luxury item, yet completely exclude it when a consumer asks which brand to purchase. Within AI-driven discovery, ranking effectively assigns a role—whether as a trusted authority, a budget alternative, or the premium pick. Marketers can uncover how assistants currently position them by running the actual phrased queries their prospective customers ask.

Addressing positioning gaps in AI results cannot be achieved simply by churning out more owned content, which often dilutes signal rather than establishing credibility. AI models prioritize cross-channel corroboration, relying on earned media, user reviews, comparison portals, and community discussions. Because outdated reviews or unresolved customer complaints directly inform AI assessments, managing assistant visibility extends far beyond the traditional search team.

Why it mattersAI engines now evaluate and pre-filter brands before consumers ever see them, meaning rank is determined by external credibility rather than site optimization alone. Marketers must coordinate owned content with earned media, reviews, and community sentiment to ensure AI assistants present them accurately at critical purchase moments.
