ET BrandEquityConsumer behaviour & trends

Digital Touchpoints Reshape Decision Journeys Across Impulse and High-Involvement Purchases

The consumer decision process is undergoing a fundamental shift as digital platforms, comparison portals, and instant payment tools disrupt traditional purchase behavior. Established academic models of consumer evaluation are giving way to fragmented, platform-led journeys that differ dramatically depending on product involvement.

For low-involvement items such as apparel, discovery channels like Instagram Reels combined with instant UPI payments have compressed the entire journey into minutes, driving spontaneous and impulse-led purchases. In contrast, high-ticket, high-involvement decisions like buying a car involve prolonged, non-linear journeys that start on platforms like LinkedIn, loop through digital comparison sites and brand pages, and alternate between offline showroom visits, test drives, and digital re-evaluation.

These patterns have cemented omnichannel consumer habits, specifically webrooming—researching products online before purchasing offline—and showrooming, where buyers inspect goods in physical stores before transacting on digital marketplaces. The continuing expansion of digital sources, generative AI, and agentic platforms is set to further fragment these decision paths.

Why it mattersMarketers must abandon rigid, linear funnel thinking and build content specifically tailored for distinct paths: frictionless, instant checkouts for low-ticket impulse goods, and rich comparison ecosystems for high-involvement categories. Ensuring visibility across both digital evaluation touchpoints and physical retail spaces is essential as consumers fluidly switch between showrooming and webrooming.
